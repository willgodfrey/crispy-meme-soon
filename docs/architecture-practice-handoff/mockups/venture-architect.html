What is a venture architect?

By Will Godfrey

Questions about the practice

A venture architect is the person accountable for the design of a new business before it exists, and for the integrity of that design through its first build.

The architect of record

I use the word architect in its building sense: one person working for the owner, accountable for how the parts fit together. The design comes before the build, the work is checked against it, and the engagement ends when the handover is complete.

A new business may have a strategist who wrote the case, a technical lead who chose the stack, lawyers who shaped the entity and a sponsor who approved the budget. Each has a part. The venture architect holds the whole.

The decisions

The design comes down to a small set of connected decisions: what the business sells, what it prices in, what each unit costs to deliver, what it buys or builds, what it inherits, who governs it and how it is funded.

These decisions sit in three layers: commercial, technical and institutional. Price may be set by one person and cost by another. The relationship with a parent may be negotiated by lawyers and lived by the product team. The joints are the architect's territory.

Each decision needs a record: the choice, the alternatives rejected, the reasons and the evidence that would cause us to change it.

Why AI changes the design

In an AI-native business, the architecture is the business model. Models, routing and human review affect the cost and quality of delivery. The choice between a system that does the work and one that helps a person do it affects the offer and the price.

These are business decisions made in technical language. They need to be made together, before the first build commits the venture to them.

One accountable seat

The venture architect works for the owner and brings no build team to sell. That independence matters when the evidence says to stop.

The role holds design authority across all three layers. It requires someone who has held the technical seat in a new business and lived with business design decisions as a founder.

Design, prove, hand over

In design, I make the decisions with the sponsor and founders. I write down the alternatives and the conditions for stopping before anyone is invested in the answer.

In prove, I hold the technical seat while the first version reaches committed customers. I report cost and quality at each gate so capital is released against evidence.

In handover, I hire the permanent technical lead and leave on the date written into the letter at the start. A fixed date gives the engagement a clear purpose: make the team capable of running what we designed.

Where the practice comes from

My work has moved from applications to enterprises to whole ventures. During nine years at McKinsey, I served as CTO of North America's first large-scale digital business build, stood up next to an industrial parent.

I have built AI systems hands-on since 2021, written my own method for standing up an AI-native venture next to a PE-backed services firm, and run two ventures of my own. The work keeps the design accountable to what it costs to live with.

Questions about the practice

Is this a fractional CTO?

The technical seat is part of the engagement. The scope is the design of the whole business: what it sells, what it costs to deliver, who governs it and when it stops. I hold the technical seat only until the design is proven and a permanent lead is in place.

How does this relate to a venture studio?

My role is to work for the owner as an independent design authority. I bring no build team to sell. If a studio or engineering team is part of the build, the same design decisions, evidence gates and stopping conditions still apply.

We already have a strategy firm. Where do you fit?

I work alongside advisory firms. My focus is the design decisions that connect the business case to a working system, and accountability through the handover to a permanent technical lead.

Can you work with the team we already have?

Yes. The commission starts with the decisions and capabilities already in place. Design authority can stand on its own, or include me holding the technical seat through the first build.

What have you built?

A new digital business next to an industrial parent, as its CTO. An AI agent now in production at an insurance brokerage. Two ventures of my own. The work page has the detail.
