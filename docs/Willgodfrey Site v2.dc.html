What is a venture architect?

A venture architect is the person accountable for the design of a new business before it exists, and for the integrity of that design through its first build.

I use the word architect in its building sense. Every major building has an architect of record. That person works for the owner and never for the contractor. They produce the drawings before money moves. They coordinate the structural, mechanical and civil engineers, whose work has to fit together. They certify progress before each payment is released. When the building is substantially complete they say so, and they leave. Their name stays on the drawings.

New businesses rarely have that person. They have a strategist who wrote the business case, a technical lead who chose the stack, lawyers who papered the entity, and a sponsor who approved the budget. Each did good work. Nobody was accountable for whether the parts fit.

01

The decisions

The design of a venture comes down to a small set of decisions. What it sells, and to whom. What unit it prices in. What each unit costs to deliver at the quality the buyer needs. What it buys, what it builds, and what it inherits from a parent company. Who owns it, and who decides what. How it is funded, in what tranches, against what evidence. Who leads it. Under what measured conditions it stops.

Every one of these is cheap to change on paper and expensive to change once the company is running. Most ventures that fail made them by default. The pricing unit was copied from the parent. People and habits drifted across because nobody drew the line. The conditions for stopping were never written, so the venture never stopped. It faded.

The decisions sit in three layers: commercial, technical and institutional. Each layer usually has a capable owner. The trouble is at the joints. Price is set by one person and cost per unit by another, and the margin is whatever falls out. What crosses over from the parent is negotiated by lawyers and lived by the product team. Who owns the customer gets decided the first time two sellers call the same account. The joints are the architect's territory.

02

Why this is a senior seat

The title already exists in the venture studio world. There it names a staff role on the business side of a build, working alongside a separate engineering team. That is a useful job, and a different one.

An architect of record is a single, senior point of accountability. There is one per venture. They work for the owner and bring no build team to sell, which is what lets them recommend stopping. They hold design authority across all three layers, because the decisions cannot be made separately. They put their name on the design. And they have held the seat before: they have been the technical lead of a new business, and they have started and stopped companies of their own.

03

Why now

For a long time a strategist could write the business case and hand it to engineers. Software margins were forgiving enough to allow it.

AI-native businesses are different, because every request carries a real cost. In ICONIQ's January 2026 State of AI survey of about 300 software executives building AI products, average gross margin on those products was 41% in 2024 and is projected at 52% for 2026. Inference runs at 23% of revenue for companies at scale. Pricing is unsettled too: more than a third of those companies expect to change how they price within a year.

So the choices an engineer makes about models, routing and human review set the gross margin. The choice between an agent that does the work and a copilot that helps someone do it sets the price. Those are business decisions made in technical language, and they are made before the first line of code. In an AI-native business, the architecture is the business model. Someone has to hold both.

04

Design, prove, hand over

My work runs in three stages.

In design, I make the decisions with the sponsor and the founders. I write each one down with the alternatives we rejected and the reasons. The conditions for stopping are written then, before anyone is invested in the answer.

In prove, I hold the technical seat while the first version ships to committed customers. I report cost and quality at each gate, so capital is released against evidence.

In handover, I hire the permanent technical lead and leave on a date we wrote into the letter at the start.

The date matters. An architect who can stay indefinitely has a reason to become necessary. One with a date has a reason to make the team capable.

05

What I do not do

I do not supply a build team. I do not serve as chief executive. I do not stay past the handover date. I do not recommend building when the evidence says stop.

06

How I came to this

I have been an architect of one kind or another for more than a decade: of applications, then of the front-office systems of large enterprises during nine years at McKinsey, then of whole ventures. At McKinsey I served as CTO of North America's first large-scale digital business build, a new company stood up next to an industrial parent.

I wrote the Carve-forward method for standing up an AI-native venture next to a PE-backed services firm. I also run two ventures of my own, which keeps me honest about what design decisions cost to live with.

07

Questions I get asked

Is this a fractional CTO?
No. A fractional CTO owns the technology and inherits everything else. I own the design of the whole business: what it sells, what it costs to deliver, who governs it, and when it stops. The technical seat is one part of that, and I hold it only until the design is proven.
I have seen this title at BCG and at venture studios. Is it the same thing?
Same words, different seat. There it names a staff role on the business side of a build, several to a venture, inside a firm that also sells the build team. I work for the owner as the architect of record. One per venture.
Why not hire a venture studio?
A studio designs and builds, often for equity, so it is architect and contractor at once. That can work. It also means nobody at the table is paid to tell you to stop. I bring no build team, and the conditions for stopping are in my drawings.
We already have a strategy firm. Why add you?
Keep them. A strategy firm tells you whether to build and sizes the prize. I make the design decisions that follow, prove them in a running system, and stay accountable until a named technical lead takes over. I work alongside advisory firms, not in place of them.
What have you built?
A new digital business next to an industrial parent, as its CTO. An AI agent now in production at a $6B insurance brokerage. Two ventures of my own. And the method I work from. The work page has the detail.
How does a commission start?
With a thirty-minute conversation. Then we look at the options for the owner, including the ones with no venture in them, before anyone commits to a build.

If you are weighing a new business next to an existing one, I am glad to talk.
