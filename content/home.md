Venture architect

The architect of record for new AI-native businesses. I design them, prove the design in a working system, and hand over on a date.

<!-- block: thesis -->
## In an AI-native business, the architecture is the business model.

The choices an engineer makes about models, routing and human review set the gross margin. The choice between an agent and a copilot sets the price. Those are business decisions made in technical language, before the first line of code. Someone has to hold both.

<!-- block: architect-of-record | image: aerialHome -->
## One per venture. Working for the owner.

A major building has one architect of record. That person works for the owner, puts their name on the drawings, and certifies the work before money is released. I do that job for a new business.

I bring no build team to sell, so I can tell you to stop. I hold the technical seat only long enough to prove the design. I leave on a date we write down at the start.

<!-- block: situations -->
## Three situations

- A sponsor wants a productized, AI-native business next to a services firm in the portfolio. The question is whether it is separable, fundable and worth more apart.
- Founders have been told to do something with AI. They want the options laid out before they agree to anything.
- A venture is already under way and the margin is not what the deck promised. Nobody can say which decision caused it.

<!-- block: decisions -->
## The decisions that are cheap on paper and expensive later

- What it sells, and to whom
- What each unit costs to deliver
- What it buys, builds and inherits
- Who owns and governs it
- How it is funded, and against what evidence
- Who leads it
- When it stops

<!-- block: stages | numbered: true -->
## Design, prove, hand over

1. Design. I make the decisions with the sponsor and the founders, and write each one down with the alternatives we rejected.
2. Prove. I hold the technical seat while the first version ships to committed customers, and report cost and quality at each gate.
3. Hand over. I hire the permanent technical lead and leave on the date in the letter.

<!-- block: two-shapes -->
## Two shapes

| | Design authority | Design authority plus the seat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you get | The design decisions, the memo for your board, the gates and the founding hires | All of that, plus me as interim technical lead |
| How long | Bounded by the design phase | Until a handover date written into the letter |
| Where | Largely remote, with scheduled on-site blocks | The same |

I take a small number of commissions each year.

<!-- block: standards -->
## Standards you can hold me to

- The owner is my client. I take nothing from anyone who builds.
- Every decision is written down with the alternatives we rejected.
- The sponsor and the founders get the same memo on the same day.
- The conditions for stopping are written before the build is funded.
- Capital is released against evidence I report at each gate.
- My exit has a date.

<!-- block: refusals -->
## What I do not do

- Supply a build team
- Serve as chief executive
- Stay past the handover date
- Recommend building when the evidence says stop
