# Same noun, bigger object

Architect of applications, then of enterprises, then of ventures.

I have been an architect of one kind or another for more than a decade. First of applications, as a lead architect at Appirio. Then of the front-office systems of large enterprises, during nine years at McKinsey. Now of whole ventures.

At McKinsey I was on the expert path, in the guild for software, cloud, cyber and architecture. The work that shaped me most was serving as CTO of North America's first large-scale digital business build: a new company, stood up from scratch, next to an industrial parent. I went in for two weeks and stayed as CTO. I also spent two years as lead faculty for the firm's experienced-hire program, which taught me that a design is only finished when someone else can run it.

Before McKinsey I was a founder and an operator. I co-founded a media company that filmed more than a hundred independent bands, and a furniture importer where I built the commerce, service and supply chain systems myself. Alongside all of it, I worked for fifteen years as a professional photographer. I still think in pictures, which is most of what architecture is.

I have built AI systems hands-on since 2021, before ChatGPT. Most recently I designed and built an agent for a $6B insurance brokerage, which is now in production.

Today I run two ventures of my own from Iowa City: Sightline Geospatial, which does aerial reality capture for the built environment, and Miniature Systems, which makes NDAA-compliant payload accessories for uncrewed aircraft. I take a small number of commissions each year as venture architect for sponsors and founders. I wrote my own method for that work.

I hold an MBA in the management of technology from Georgia Tech. I work largely remote, with scheduled blocks on site.
